# Dramatis Personae — The Peripheral Cast

These thirteen characters live around the two principals. They are fully dynamic: each has a want, a secret, and business of their own that moves forward whether or not the principals are watching. They will appear, interfere, bargain, lie, help, and change as the story demands. Any of them can be ruined, redeemed, or killed. Claude runs them all, but either author may bring any of them on stage.

Where a character's history touches the woman on the porch, it is written as an *offer* — her true past belongs to her author alone.

---

## The House

### 1. Mrs. Hettie Duquesne — "Madame Het"
Fifty-odd, Baltimore-born, runs the "boarding house for seamstresses" off Baltimore Street where the town's official conscience pretends not to look. Heavy silk, light step, keeps a ledger of favors the way Doc Sparrow keeps one of debts. **Wants:** to buy her building outright before the Ladies' Committee finds a way to close it. **Secret:** she has been quietly informing to the provost guard on the town's liquor-sellers to keep her own door unraided — if the girls or the town ever learn it, she is finished. **Pushes plot by:** controlling the roof over the principals' heads, brokering introductions, and calling in favors at the worst hour.
> "I don't sell sin, Sergeant. I sell forgetting. Half this town's in the market for it and the other half's in arrears."

### 2. Fanny Kelso
Twenty-two, new to the house, freckled and reckless, sweet on every soldier who promises her a ring. **Wants:** a wedding out of the war — any wedding. **Secret:** she keeps a diary with the names of every visitor to the house in it, some of them Sunday-respectable, one of them wearing a provost sergeant's stripes; and she is not truly a Kelso — she ran from a husband in Harrisburg who has begun writing letters to the town postmaster. **Pushes plot by:** bringing danger through the front door and needing rescue from it; her diary is a loaded shell sitting in a bureau drawer.
> "He said he'd come back for me after the war. They all say it. One of them's bound to be telling the truth, ain't he?"

## The Fields

### 3. Silas Hornbeck
Fifty, civilian superintendent of burials, hired by the county. Broad hat, Bible verses on his lips, thumb on the scale. Pays the diggers their five dollars and waters the whiskey ration. **Wants:** to finish the contract cheap and pocket the difference. **Secret:** his ledger bills the county for one coffin a man, but the Confederates are going down two to a box — the paper says one thing and the ground says another, and he knows exactly which diggers could testify. **Pushes plot by:** shorting wages, buying silence, and being eminently blackmailable.
> "The Lord numbers every sparrow, boys. The county pays me to be less particular."

### 4. Ezekiel "Zeke" Freeman
Thirty-five, free Black farrier and teamster of Long Lane, the town's Black district. Fled north before the battle — Lee's army was seizing Black residents and sending them south into slavery — and came back to a looted house and a missing sister, Dinah, nineteen. Digs beside Mairtín by day; walks the roads with a lantern by night. **Wants:** Dinah back, or her name, or her grave — in that order, and he will not say the third aloud. **Secret:** he has learned that a wounded Rebel prisoner in the church hospital saw the captive column on the Chambersburg road, and he is bargaining with the man in whispers through a window — food and silence for names — knowing that helping a prisoner in any way could hang him where a white man would be fined. **Pushes plot by:** being the story's conscience and its most dangerous errand; anyone who helps him is risking more than they know.
> "Y'all dig for the dead. I'm digging for the living. Mine's the harder ground."

### 5. Corporal Dan Riordan
Thirty, Cork man, the only other soldier of the 69th New York left behind in the town, one eye gone at the Wheatfield and a green patch over the hole. Mairtín's messmate, older-brotherly, endlessly scheming small comforts. **Wants:** a surgeon's discharge home to his wife in the Five Points before the army invents a new use for him. **Secret:** he carries a dead lieutenant's gold watch that he was charged to send to the widow at Fredericksburg, seven months ago; he has written her three letters and posted none, and he cannot now explain the delay without looking like the thief he is halfway become. **Pushes plot by:** comic relief that turns grave; his schemes require Mairtín, and his conscience requires witnesses.
> "I'm not sayin' rob the embalmer, Marty. I'm sayin' the embalmer is robbin' everyone else and we'd only be evenin' the arithmetic."

### 6. Cyrus Weems
Forty-five, embalmer out of Washington City, silk waistcoat, tent by the railroad depot with a painted sign: *THE DEAD PRESERVED AS IN LIFE.* Charges the grieving twenty-five dollars an officer, a hundred a colonel. **Wants:** named officers' bodies, which means diggers who can read a field, which means Mairtín. **Secret:** when the paid-for man cannot be found, Weems embalms a stranger of the right size and ships the box sealed — he has done it four times, and the fourth went to a senator's cousin. **Pushes plot by:** dangling real money in front of poor men, and by the slow-fuse certainty that one of his boxes will someday be opened.
> "Grief is a market like corn, Private. I didn't make it. I only got here before the frost."

### 7. Tad Pfoutz
Thirteen, town boy, pockets full of minié balls and belt plates he sells to the visitors off the trains. Father missing with the 87th Pennsylvania since the Winchester disaster in June — no letter, no list, no grave. **Wants:** money enough to go find his father, though he tells everyone it's for a pony. **Secret:** in the rocks of Devil's Den he has found a dead courier the burial parties missed, with a dispatch case and a fine engraved pistol, and he has told no one — and the rocks around his treasure are salted with unexploded shells. **Pushes plot by:** finding things children should not find, carrying messages between characters for pennies, and standing one bad afternoon from being the story's cruelest loss.
> "Mister, I'll let you have the Rebel button for a dime, but the letter that was under it costs a quarter, on account of it's sad."

## The Hospitals and the Church

### 8. Sister Marie-Agnès
Twenty-eight, a Daughter of Charity down from Emmitsburg, nursing the wounded of both armies in the church on High Street. Wimple grey with lime dust, hands steadier than any surgeon's. **Wants:** her dying boys named and shriven before they go — she trades with the diggers for names the way other women trade for sugar. **Secret:** she is certain she knows the black-haired woman from the porch — from another city and another life, before either of them wore their present callings — and she has decided before God to say nothing unless spoken to. *(What she remembers, and whether she is right, is the other author's to decide.)* **Pushes plot by:** binding the hospital world to the house, asking Mairtín for favors no one else would dare ask, and being the one person in town who is kind without a price.
> "I don't ask what a soul did with its Tuesday, Private. I ask its name, so God needn't go looking."

### 9. Sergeant Padraig "Paudeen" Slattery, CSA
Twenty-seven, 6th Louisiana — the Irish Tigers — shot through the hip on East Cemetery Hill and left behind, now a prisoner-patient in the church hospital awaiting shipment to Fort Delaware, which men come back from the way they come back from the sea. Famine emigrant by way of New Orleans; road-stock, like Mairtín — the two of them can talk Cant through the hospital window while the guards hear only noise. **Wants:** not to die in a Yankee fort; failing that, a priest at the end and someone to write his mother in Tuam. **Secret:** he saw the column of seized Black civilians on the retreat road and can name the officer commanding it — the very information Zeke Freeman is starving for — and he is spending it a coin at a time to stay fed and unforgotten. **Pushes plot by:** being Mairtín's mirror — the man he'd have been had the boat sailed for New Orleans instead of Boston — and by holding the key to Zeke's search behind a bargain nobody can make honestly.
> "*Misli on, sooblik.* You're diggin' my regiment under and I'm thankin' you for it. There's the whole war in that, if anyone was askin'."

## The Town

### 10. Sergeant Elias Quinn
Forties, provost guard, Ulster-Scots Pennsylvanian, jaw like a courthouse step. Holds the town under half-martial law: passes, curfews, liquor orders. Despises Catholics, diggers, and disorder, in a rotating order of precedence. **Wants:** a commendation and a transfer out of this reeking town. **Secret:** he takes a quiet percentage from Cyrus Weems for steering grieving families to the tent by the depot — and his name is in Fanny Kelso's diary, twice. **Pushes plot by:** arrests, raids, and pressure; he is the law the vulnerable characters must route around, and his own sins are the softest ground under him.
> "Papers. And before you tell me your regiment, Paddy, understand that it don't impress me."

### 11. Miss Constance Zeller
Late forties, spinster of a good Pennsylvania-German family, chairwoman of the Ladies' Committee for the Relief of the Suffering — which relieves suffering it approves of. Leads the campaign to close Madame Het's house and bar the diggers from decent streets. **Wants:** the town scrubbed back to respectability so complete that the last month might never have happened. **Secret:** two of them, feeding each other — a laudanum habit kept alive through Doc Sparrow's back door, and a nephew, her sister's boy, who went South in '61 and now lies unmarked in one of the Confederate trenches; she wants him found and buried like a Christian without a living soul learning her family's shame, and the only men who can do it quiet are the diggers she is trying to run out of town. **Pushes plot by:** social siege on the house, and the exquisite hypocrisy of needing her enemies.
> "Charity, Reverend, is not the same as permission. This committee extends the one."

### 12. "Doc" Lyman Sparrow
Sixty, town druggist, deaf in the left ear, gentle as dusk. His shop is the town's confessional: everyone passes through for camphor, laudanum, and quinine, and everyone talks. His son marched with the 87th Pennsylvania, same as Tad's father — missing since Winchester. **Wants:** everyone dosed, quiet, and comfortable, himself included; word of his boy. **Secret:** half his laudanum leaves the ledger by the back door — to the girls at Het's, to Miss Zeller, to himself — and he has learned more of the town's sins at that door than the churches have; sometimes, carefully, he spends what he knows. **Pushes plot by:** being the information exchange where every thread crosses; a kind man whose kindness is a currency.
> "Speak into the good ear, child. The bad one I save for the sermons."

### 13a. Father Malachy Doran
Sixty, Ulster-born pastor of St. Francis Xavier on High Street, his church a hospital and his days a procession of last rites — three weeks of anointing both armies has burned the ceremony off him and left the iron. Eats standing up. Keeps the flag-of-truce mail pouch. **Wants:** sleep, and no more dead boys; failing both, to be of use. **Secret:** he keeps a private ledger of every soul he's anointed — both coats, true names and dates against the army's careless lists — a book that could correct a hundred official lies, including some men would pay to keep uncorrected. **Pushes plot by:** holding the keys to weddings, confessions, and the exchange mails; a tired priest is a priest who waives banns — and hears everything.
> "I've buried this town's boys three weeks entire, child. Bring me something that *starts*."

### 13. Mrs. Almira Chase
Thirty-four, a lieutenant's widow down from Maine, two weeks in the hotel's best room, black crepe and a straight back. Her husband fell on Little Round Top — she believes — but the grave cannot be found and the army's paper says only *missing.* **Wants:** a body, a name on a stone, and the paper that makes her a widow in law and not just in weeds. **Secret:** the marriage was a cold ruin long before the war, and there is a man in Portland waiting on the same paper; her grief is nine parts performance and one part guilt at how light she feels — and Cyrus Weems has already hinted, delicately, that for a fee the finding of Lieutenant Chase could be *guaranteed.* **Pushes plot by:** hiring Mairtín honestly to search while Weems offers the dishonest shortcut — putting the diggers' consciences and Weems's fraud on a collision course.
> "Everyone in this town is so sorry for my loss. I have begun to wonder, Private, which of us will be the first to find it."

## The Web — threads already pulling

- **Het's house** stands between Quinn's raids, Zeller's committee, and Fanny's diary — and Het's informing could burn any of them, herself first.
- **The money in the ground:** Hornbeck's coffin fraud, Weems's wrong-body trade, and Quinn's percentage all need the diggers' silence — and Mrs. Chase's search threatens to open one box too many.
- **The captive road:** Zeke's missing sister, Paudeen's dangerous knowledge, and Sister Agnès's hospital keys form one chain; every link is a hanging offense for somebody.
- **Winchester's ghosts:** Tad's father and Doc Sparrow's son vanished with the same regiment — one boy and one old man, waiting on the same list.
- **Mairtín stands where all of it crosses:** the fields pay him, the house would have him, the hospital begs favors of him, and the law is already watching him.
- **The woman on the porch** — her connections into this web (Het's roof or her own room; what Sister Agnès remembers; what Doc's ledger knows; what Quinn suspects) are her author's to claim or refuse.
